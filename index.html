Glare / overexposure

New to this? A step-by-step guide to glare and overexposed boards

What the problem looks like

A ChArUco board is a chessboard whose white squares each hold a small black-and-white ArUco marker. The app finds the markers first, reads each marker's ID from its pattern of black and white cells, and only then places the chessboard corners (the green dots) between the markers it recognised. If a marker is not recognised, the corners around it are lost too.

When the board is overexposed (too bright), the white parts of the board are blown out to pure white and light bleeds into the black cells next to them, so the markers look "fat" and blurry. A glare hotspot is the same thing in one spot: a shiny reflection of a lamp on the board. In both cases the marker patterns can no longer be read, and whole regions of a clearly visible board get no green dots.

Step 1 — the best fix is at recording time

No detector setting can recover detail that was recorded as pure white. If you can re-record, do that first:

  • Lower the camera's exposure time and/or gain until the board's white squares are light grey rather than pure white. A slightly dark board detects better than a bright one.
  • Avoid direct reflections: move or diffuse the lights, or keep the board at an angle where no lamp reflects into a camera. A matte (non-glossy, non-laminated) board print helps a lot.
  • Check every camera: one camera is often brighter than the others because it faces the light.

Step 2 — check whether you need glare handling

  1. Load your videos and set the board above (squares, marker size and dictionary must match your printed board — a wrong dictionary looks just like a detection failure).
  2. Go to a frame where the board is clearly visible in a camera that looks bright, and press Detect current frame (D).
  3. Look at the green dots on the video and at the counts next to the button (markers / corners per camera). If a camera finds far fewer corners than the others, or a clearly visible part of the board — especially the brightest part — has no green dots, glare handling will probably help.

After each change press D again on the same frame and compare the counts. Stop climbing when a level no longer adds corners: higher is not automatically better. Then try two or three other bright frames, and frames that were fine before, to make sure nothing got worse.

Step 4 — run detection and check the calibration

  1. Run batch detection. The setting applies to the whole run; detections that were already computed are not updated until you run batch detection again.
  2. In the Detections table and strip, the bright camera should now have corner counts closer to the other cameras.
  3. Continue with intrinsics (stage 3) and extrinsics (stage 4) as usual. If the per-camera error or the cross-view reprojection error gets clearly worse than with a lower level, go back down a level.

Custom settings

Choosing a level fills in the four controls; changing any control switches to Custom. Hover over each control for details. Cell margin is the main fix (0.3 is a good value; 0.13 is OpenCV's default). Board-guided recovery and highlight gamma are small extra gains. Min markers / corner = 1 trades a little accuracy for more corners.

The chosen settings are written to the log for every detection run and saved in the session file.
